L36@P1: However, if one considers that a network containing n neurons is capable of

L747@P18: 6. Conclusion
L748@P18: The results in this paper amount to a limitation on the power of neural nets. In
L749@P18: spite of the current climate of optimism, and in spite of successes in limited
L750@P18: categories of computation, one cannot look to neural networks as the parallel
L751@P18: panacea of the future.
L752@P18: The limitations demonstrated here for the classic McCullough- Pitts networks
L753@P18: apply with some force, obviously, to the case of connectionist mission. For
L754@P18: example, a Hopfield-style network (see Hopfield and Tank, [5]), having as its
L755@P18: goal a solution of the traveling salesman problem amounts to a finite automaton
L756@P18: in which all states (hopefully) lead to one or just a few states.
L757@P18: We saw that while some m-state machines can be built using very few (in the
L758@P18: order of log m) neurons, some need many more (namely, K(m)) neurons to be
L759@P18: built. A naturally arising question is whether there are any interesting classes of
L760@P18: finite automata that can be efficiently built as neural nets; namely, with a
L761@P18: number of neurons that is very small compared with the size of the automata.
L762@P18: Preliminary research by two of the authors of this paper indicates that while
L763@P18: there are classes of finite state machines that can be efficiently simulated in this
L764@P18: sense, all really interesting classes considered so far are too rich in structure
L765@P18: and size. This research is continuing.
L766@P18: In addition, there is a large number of technical questions about the bounds
L767@P18: presented in this paper. For example, we suspect (see Theorem 4.4) that a
L768@P18: limitation on the Fan-in of neurons may lead to m-state machines that can be
L769@P18: simulated only by a superlinear (in m) number of neurons.
