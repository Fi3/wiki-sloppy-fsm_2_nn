L7: ## Abstract
L8: Neural networks are known to be universal computers for Boolean functions. Recent advancements in hardware have significantly reduced matrix multiplication times, making neural network simulation both fast and efficient. Consequently, functions defined by complex Boolean networks are increasingly viable candidates for simulation through their neural network representation. Prior research has introduced a general method for deriving neural network representations of Boolean networks.
L9: However, the resulting neural networks are often suboptimal in terms of the number of neurons and connections, leading to slower simulation performance. Optimizing them while preserving functional equivalence --lossless optimization-- is an NP-hard problem, and current methods only provide lossy solutions. In this paper, we present a deterministic algorithm to optimize such neural networks in terms of neurons and connections while preserving functional equivalence.
L10: Moreover, to accelerate the compression of the neural network, we introduce an objective-aware algorithm that exploits representations that are shared among subproblems of the overall optimization. We demonstrate experimentally that we are able to reduce connections and neurons by up to 70% and 60%, respectively, in comparison to state-of-the-art.
L11: We also find that our objective-aware algorithm results in consistent speedups in optimization time, achieving up to 34.3x and 5.9x speedup relative to naive and caching solutions, respectively. Our methods are of practical relevance to applications such as high-throughput circuit simulation and placing neurosymbolic systems on the same hardware architecture.

L28@P0: 1 INTRODUCTION
L29@P0: Universal approximation theorems (Hornik et al., 1989) and Turing-completeness (Siegelmann &
L30@P0: Sontag, 1992) provide the theoretical foundation for implementing computable functions with neural
L31@P0: networks (NNs). At present, the predominant methods for implementing functions with NNs are
L32@P0: learning-based techniques, which rely on optimization algorithms, data, and compute to elicit a
L33@P0: function of interest (LeCun et al., 2015). While these techniques provide a solution for obtaining
L34@P0: functions that are difficult to programmatically specify, they come at the cost of unsustainable energy
L35@P0: usage (Strubell et al., 2019), ever-longer training times, and safety concerns due to the current lack of
L36@P0: understanding surrounding functions derived from learning-based techniques (Qi et al., 2023).
L37@P0: Another much less prevalent implementation method for NNs is to synthesize a network from a known
L38@P0: functional specification. The specification may be in the form of an explicit construction, detailing
L39@P0: the network structure, neurons, and connections (Hewitt et al., 2020; Karuvally et al., 2024), or it
L40@P0: may be provided in a programming language, requiring a method to convert the specification into a
L41@P0: resulting NN (Siegelmann, 1994; Gruau et al., 1995; Neto et al., 2003). These implementation methods
L42@P0: generally require significantly less time and energy in comparison to learning-based techniques and
L43@P0: provide guarantees on NN functionality which are critical for safety and security.
L44@P0: One such programmatic implementation method is that of NN-based technology mapping (Patel et al.,
L45@P0: 2022; Gavier et al., 2023), the problem of finding a NN that is functionally equivalent to a Boolean
L46@P0: network (BN) (i.e., ∀x ∈ {0, 1}
L47@P0: n, NN(x) = BN(x)). This method holds significant potential for

L431@P3: In this section we set out to achieve the two goals stated in the introduction: (1) optimizing the NN size and (2) doing so efficiently. We begin
L432@P3: by considering the problem of optimizing NN size while maintaining

L437@P3: we identify sub-NN optimization as a constrained minimization problem,

L440@P3: aggregate. Since we pose NN optimization as minimizing various sub-NNs,

L443@P3: into classes, the solution for a representative of the class can be used to
L444@P3: accelerate the acquisition of the solutions for all other members of the class.
