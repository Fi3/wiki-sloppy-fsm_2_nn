L26: # Notes on Equivalence and Minimization of Weighted Automata
L27: 
L28: Stefan Kiefer
L29: ###### Abstract
L30: 
L31: This set of notes re-proves known results on weighted automata (over a field, also known as multiplicity automata). The text offers a unified view on theorems and proofs that have appeared in the literature over decades and were written in different styles and contexts. None of the results reported here are claimed to be new.
L32: 
L33: The content centres around fundamentals of equivalence and minimization, with an emphasis on algorithmic aspects.
L34: The presentation is minimalistic. No attempt has been made to motivate the material. Weighted automata are viewed from a linear-algebra angle. As a consequence, the proofs, which are meant to be succinct, but complete and almost self-contained, rely mainly on elementary linear algebra.
L35: ## 1 Preliminaries
L36: 
L37: Let $\mathbb{K}$ be a field. When speaking about algorithms and computational complexity, we will implicitly take as $\mathbb{K}$ the field $\mathbb{Q}$ of rational numbers (where we assume that rational numbers are encoded as quotients of integers encoded in binary). For a finite alphabet $\Sigma$ we call a map $s:\Sigma^{*}\to\mathbb{K}$ a series.
L38: An automaton $\mathcal{A}=(n,\Sigma,M,\alpha,\eta)$ consists of a natural number $n$ (to which we refer as the number of states), a finite alphabet $\Sigma$, a map $M:\Sigma\to\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}$, an initial (row) vector $\alpha\in\mathbb{K}^{n}$, and a final (column) vector $\eta\in\mathbb{K}^{n}$.
L39: Extend $M$ to a monoid homomorphism $M:\Sigma^{*}\to\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}$ by setting $M(a_{1}\cdots a_{k}):=M(a_{1})\cdots M(a_{k})$ and $M(\varepsilon):=I_{n}$, where $\varepsilon$ is the empty word and $I_{n}\in\{0,1\}^{n\times n}$ the $n\times n$ identity matrix. The semantics of an automaton $\mathcal{A}$ is the series $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket:\Sigma^{*}\to\mathbb{K}$ with $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket(w)=\alpha M(w)\eta$.
L40: Automata $\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}$ over the same alphabet $\Sigma$ are said to be equivalent if $\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{1}\rrbracket=\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{2}\rrbracket$. An automaton $\mathcal{A}$ is minimal if there is no equivalent automaton $\mathcal{A}^{\prime}$ with fewer states. If $n=0$, it is natural to put $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket(w)=0$ for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$.
L41: We have the following closure properties:
L42: ###### Proposition 1.1.
L43: Let $\mathcal{A}_{i}=(n_{i},\Sigma,M_{i},\alpha_{i},\eta_{i})$ for $i\in\{1,2\}$ be automata.
L44: One can compute in logarithmic space (hence, in polynomial time) automata $\mathcal{A}_{+},\mathcal{A}_{-},\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}$ with $\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{+}\rrbracket(w)=\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{1}\rrbracket(w)+\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{2}\rrbracket(w)$ and $\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{-}\rrbracket(w)=\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{1}\rrbracket(w)-\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{2}\rrbracket(w)$ and $\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}\rrbracket(w)=\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{1}\rrbracket(w)\cdot\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{2}\rrbracket(w)$ for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$.
L45: One can compute $\mathcal{A}_{+},\mathcal{A}_{-}$ with $O(|\Sigma|(n_{1}+n_{2})^{2})$ arithmetic operations. One can compute $\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}$ with $O(|\Sigma|n_{1}^{2}n_{2}^{2})$ arithmetic operations.
L46: ###### Proof.
L47: 
L48: It is straightforward to check that the automaton $\mathcal{A}_{+}=(n_{1}+n_{2},\Sigma,M_{+},(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}),\eta_{+})$ with
L49: 
L50:  | $$M_{+}(a)\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}M_{1}(a)&0_{n_{1},n_{2}}\\
L51: 0_{n_{2},n_{1}}&M_{2}(a)\end{pmatrix}\quad\text{for all $a\in\Sigma$}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta_{+}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}\eta_{1}\\
L52: \eta_{2}\end{pmatrix}$$  |
L53: 
L54: is the desired automaton, where $0_{m,n}$ stands for the $m\times n$ zero matrix.
L55: The automaton $\mathcal{A}_{-}$ can be constructed similarly to $\mathcal{A}_{+}$, but $(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})$ is replaced with $(\alpha_{1},-\alpha_{2})$.
L56: Let $\mathord{\otimes}$ denote the Kronecker product. Define $\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}=(n_{1}n_{2},\Sigma,M_{\otimes},(\alpha_{1}\otimes\alpha_{2}),(\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2}))$, where $M_{\otimes}(a)=M_{1}(a)\otimes M_{2}(a)$ for all $a\in\Sigma$. Using the mixed-product property of $\mathord{\otimes}$ (i.e., $(AB)\otimes(CD)=(A\otimes C)(B\otimes D)$), we have for all $a_{1}\cdots a_{k}\in\Sigma^{*}$:
L57:  | $\displaystyle\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{\otimes}\rrbracket(w)\ $  | $\displaystyle=\ (\alpha_{1}\otimes\alpha_{2})(M_{1}(a_{1})\otimes M_{2}(a_{1}))\cdots(M_{1}(a_{k})\otimes M_{2}(a_{k}))(\eta_{1}\otimes\eta_{2})$  |
L58:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ (\alpha_{1}M_{1}(a_{1})\cdots M_{1}(a_{k})\eta_{1})\otimes(\alpha_{2}M_{2}(a_{1})\cdots M_{2}(a_{k})\eta_{2})$  |
L59:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ \llbracket\mathcal{A}_{1}\rrbracket(a_{1}\cdots a_{k})\cdot\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{2}\rrbracket(a_{1}\cdots a_{k})\qed$  |
L60: For a set $V$ of vectors we use the notation $\langle v\mid v\in V\rangle$ to denote the vector space spanned by $V$. For an automaton $\mathcal{A}$, define its forward space as the (row) vector space $\langle\alpha M(w)\mid w\in\Sigma^{*}\rangle$. Similarly, the backward space of $\mathcal{A}$ is the (column) vector space $\langle M(w)\eta\mid w\in\Sigma^{*}\rangle$.
L61: Let $s:\Sigma^{*}\to\mathbb{K}$. The Hankel matrix of $s$ is the (infinite) matrix $H\in\mathbb{K}^{\Sigma^{*}\times\Sigma^{*}}$ with $H[x,y]=s(xy)$ for all $x,y\in\Sigma^{*}$. Define $\textup{rank}(s):=\textup{rank}(H)$.
L62: ## 2 Equivalence Checking
L63: 
L64: First we discuss how to efficiently compute a basis of the forward space $\mathcal{F}:=\langle\alpha M(w)\mid w\in\Sigma^{*}\rangle$ of an automaton $\mathcal{A}=(n,\Sigma,M,\alpha,\eta)$. It is a matter of basic linear algebra to check that $\mathcal{F}$ is the smallest vector space that contains $\alpha$ and is closed under post-multiplication of $M(a)$ (i.e., $\mathcal{F}M(a)\subseteq\mathcal{F}$) for all $a\in\Sigma$. Hence cite13†Algorithm 1 computes a basis of $\mathcal{F}$.

L78: cite13†Algorithm 1 actually computes a set $W$ of words such that $\{\alpha M(w)\mid w\in W\}$ is a basis of the forward space $\mathcal{F}$. L108: cite14†Algorithm 2 of cite14†Algorithm 2 requires a check for linear independence. Using Gaussian elimination, such a check can be carried out with $O(n^{3})$ arithmetic operations. L111: ###### Proposition 2.1.
L112: 
L113: Let $\mathcal{A}=(n,\Sigma,M,\alpha,\eta)$ be an automaton. One can compute in polynomial time (with $O(|\Sigma|n^{3})$ arithmetic operations) a set $\{(w_{1},v_{1}),\ldots,(w_{\overrightarrow{n}},v_{\overrightarrow{n}})\}\subseteq\Sigma^{\leq\overrightarrow{n}-1}\times\mathbb{K}^{n}$ such that $\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{\overrightarrow{n}}\}$ is a basis of $\mathcal{F}$ and $v_{i}=\alpha M(w_{i})$ holds for all $1\leq i\leq\overrightarrow{n}$.
L114: An automaton $\mathcal{A}$ is called zero if $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket(w)=0$ for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$. We show:
L115: ###### Proposition 2.2.
L116: 
L117: Let $\mathcal{A}=(n,\Sigma,M,\alpha,\eta)$ be an automaton. One can check in polynomial time (with $O(|\Sigma|n^{3})$ arithmetic operations) whether $\mathcal{A}$ is zero, and if it is not, output $w\in\Sigma^{*}$ with $|w|\leq n-1$ such that $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket(w)\neq 0$.
L118: ###### Proof.
L119: Automaton $\mathcal{A}=(n,\Sigma,M,\alpha,\eta)$ is zero if and only if its forward space $\mathcal{F}:=\langle\alpha M(w)\mid w\in\Sigma^{*}\rangle$ is orthogonal to $\eta$, i.e., $v\eta=0$ for all $v\in\mathcal{F}$. Let $S\subseteq\mathbb{K}^{n}$ (with $|S|\leq n$) be a basis of $\mathcal{F}$. Then $\mathcal{A}$ is zero if and only if $v\eta=0$ holds for all $v\in S$. But by cite15†Proposition 2.1 one can compute such $S$. Similarly, one can compute, if it exists, the “counterexample” $w$. ∎
L120: ###### Theorem 2.3.
L121: 
L122: Let $\mathcal{A}_{i}=(n_{i},\Sigma,M_{i},\alpha_{i},\eta_{i})$ for $i\in\{1,2\}$ be automata. One can check in polynomial time (with $O(|\Sigma|(n_{1}+n_{2})^{3})$ arithmetic operations) whether $\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}$ are equivalent, and if they are not, output $w\in\Sigma^{*}$ with $|w|\leq n_{1}+n_{2}-1$ such that $\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{1}\rrbracket(w)\neq\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{2}\rrbracket(w)$.
L123: ###### Proof.
L124: 
L125: Compute automaton $\mathcal{A}_{-}$ from cite16†Proposition 1.1 . Then the theorem follows from cite17†Proposition 2.2 . ∎
L126: ### Bibliographic Remarks
L127: Equivalence checking goes back to the seminal paper by Schützenberger from 1961 [cite18†21 ]. A polynomial-time algorithm could be derived from there but was not made explicit. The books by Paz [cite19†20 ] and Eilenberg [cite20†11 ] from 1971 and 1974, respectively, describe an exponential-time algorithm based on the fact that shortest “counterexamples” have length at most $n_{1}+n_{2}-1$.
L128: An $O(|\Sigma|(n_{1}+n_{2})^{4})$ (in terms of arithmetic operations) algorithm was explicitly provided in 1992 by Tzeng [cite21†22 ]. Improvements to $O(|\Sigma|(n_{1}+n_{2})^{3})$ were then (re-)discovered, e.g., in [cite22†9 , cite23†16 , cite24†3 ]. These improvements are all based on the idea described before cite15†Proposition 2.1 . The abstract of the 2002 paper [cite25†1 ] indicates that this improvement was already known to some. Incidentally, a different algorithm, also cubic in $n$, was proposed in [cite25†1 ].
L129: ## 3 Minimization
L130: Let $\mathcal{A}=(n,\Sigma,M,\alpha,\eta)$ be an automaton. Let $F\in\mathbb{K}^{\overrightarrow{n}\times n}$ with $\overrightarrow{n}\leq n$ be a matrix whose rows form a basis of the forward space $\mathcal{F}$. Similarly, let $B\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times\overleftarrow{n}}$ with $\overleftarrow{n}\leq n$ be a matrix whose columns form a basis of the backward space $\mathcal{B}$.
L131: Since $\mathcal{F}M(a)\subseteq\mathcal{F}$ and $M(a)\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{B}$ for all $a\in\Sigma$, there exist maps $\overrightarrow{M}:\Sigma\to\mathbb{K}^{\overrightarrow{n}\times\overrightarrow{n}}$ and $\overleftarrow{M}:\Sigma\to\mathbb{K}^{\overleftarrow{n}\times\overleftarrow{n}}$ such that
L132:  | $$FM(a)\ =\ \overrightarrow{M}(a)F\quad\text{and}\quad M(a)B\ =\ B\overleftarrow{M}(a)\quad\text{for all $a\in\Sigma$.}$$  |
L133: 
L134: These maps $\overrightarrow{M},\overleftarrow{M}$ are unique, as $F,B$ have full rank. The above equalities extend inductively to words:
L135: 
L136:  | $$FM(w)\ =\ \overrightarrow{M}(w)F\quad\text{and}\quad M(w)B\ =\ B\overleftarrow{M}(w)\quad\text{for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$}$$  |  | (1)
L137: Let $\overrightarrow{\alpha}\in\mathbb{K}^{\overrightarrow{n}}$ be the unique row vector with $\overrightarrow{\alpha}F=\alpha$, and $\overleftarrow{\eta}\in\mathbb{K}^{\overleftarrow{n}}$ be the unique column vector with $B\overleftarrow{\eta}=\eta$.
L138: Call $\overrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}:=(\overrightarrow{n},\Sigma,\overrightarrow{M},\overrightarrow{\alpha},F\eta)$ the forward conjugate of $\mathcal{A}$ with base $F$, and $\overleftarrow{\mathcal{A}}:=(\overleftarrow{n},\Sigma,\overleftarrow{M},\alpha B,\overleftarrow{\eta})$ the backward conjugate of $\mathcal{A}$ with base $B$.
L139: ###### Proposition 3.1.
L140: 
L141: Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an automaton. Then $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket=\llbracket\overrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}\rrbracket=\llbracket\overleftarrow{\mathcal{A}}\rrbracket$.
L142: ###### Proof.
L143: 
L144: By symmetry, it suffices to show the first equality. Indeed, we have for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$:
L145: 
L146:  | $\displaystyle\llbracket\overrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}\rrbracket(w)\ $  | $\displaystyle=\ \overrightarrow{\alpha}\overrightarrow{M}(w)F\eta$  |
L147:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ \overrightarrow{\alpha}FM(w)\eta$  |  | by cite26†1 |
L148:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ \alpha M(w)\eta$  |  | definition of $\overrightarrow{\alpha}$  |
L149:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ \llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket(w)$  |  | ∎

L167: ###### Proposition 3.3.
L168: 
L169: Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an automaton. Then $\textup{rank}(\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket)\leq n$.
L170: ###### Proof.
L171: Consider the matrices $\widehat{F}:\mathbb{K}^{\Sigma^{*}\times n}$ and $\widehat{B}:\mathbb{K}^{n\times\Sigma^{*}}$ with $\widehat{F}[w,\cdot]=\alpha M(w)$ and $\widehat{B}[\cdot,w]=M(w)\eta$ for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$. Note that $\textup{rank}(\widehat{F})\leq n$ (and similarly $\textup{rank}(\widehat{B})\leq n$). Let $x,y\in\Sigma^{*}$.
L172: Then $(\widehat{F}\widehat{B})[x,y]=\alpha M(x)M(y)\eta=\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket(xy)$, so $\widehat{F}\widehat{B}$ is the Hankel matrix of $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket$. Hence $\textup{rank}(\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket)=\textup{rank}(\widehat{F}\widehat{B})\leq\textup{rank}(\widehat{F})\leq n$. ∎
L173: Call an automaton with $n$ states forward-minimal (resp., backward-minimal) if its forward (resp., backward) space has dimension $n$.

L187:

L198: ###### Theorem 3.6.
L199: 
L200: Let $\mathcal{A}$ be an automaton. Let $\mathcal{A}^{\prime}$ be a backward conjugate of a forward conjugate of $\mathcal{A}$ (or a forward conjugate of a backward conjugate of $\mathcal{A}$). Then $\mathcal{A}^{\prime}$ is minimal and equivalent to $\mathcal{A}$. It can be computed in polynomial time (with $O(|\Sigma|n^{3})$ arithmetic operations).
L201: ###### Proof.
L202: 
L203: Minimality follows from cite28†Propositions 3.4 and cite29†3.5 . Equivalence follows from cite30†Proposition 3.1 . Polynomial-time computability follows by invoking cite31†Proposition 3.2 twice. ∎
L204: 
L205: Let $\mathcal{A}_{i}=(n,\Sigma,M_{i},\alpha_{i},\eta_{i})$ for $i\in\{1,2\}$ be minimal, where $\mathcal{A}_{2}$ is the forward conjugate of $\mathcal{A}_{1}$ with some base $Q\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}$. By minimality and cite30†Proposition 3.1 , matrix $Q$ is invertible. Since
L206:  | $$\alpha_{2}Q=\alpha_{1},\quad\eta_{2}=Q\eta_{1},\quad QM_{1}(a)=M_{2}(a)Q\quad\text{for all }a\in\Sigma\,,$$  |
L207: 
L208: automaton $\mathcal{A}_{1}$ is the backward conjugate of $\mathcal{A}_{2}$ with base $Q$. Since
L209: 
L210:  | $$\alpha_{2}=\alpha_{1}Q^{-1},\quad Q^{-1}\eta_{2}=\eta_{1},\quad M_{1}(a)Q^{-1}=Q^{-1}M_{2}(a)\quad\text{for all }a\in\Sigma\,,$$  |
L211: automaton $\mathcal{A}_{1}$ is the forward conjugate of $\mathcal{A}_{2}$ with base $Q^{-1}$, and $\mathcal{A}_{2}$ is the backward conjugate of $\mathcal{A}_{1}$ with base $Q^{-1}$.
L212: 
L213: This motivates the following definition. Call minimal automata $\mathcal{A}_{1},\mathcal{A}_{2}$ conjugate if one is a forward (equivalently, backward) conjugate of the other.
L214: ###### Theorem 3.7.
L215: 
L216: Two minimal automata are conjugate if and only if they are equivalent.
L217: ###### Proof.
L218: 
L219: The forward direction follows from cite30†Proposition 3.1 .
L220: Towards the backward direction, let $\mathcal{A}_{i}=(n,\Sigma,M_{i},\alpha_{i},\eta_{i})$ for $i\in\{1,2\}$ be minimal equivalent automata. For $i\in\{1,2\}$, consider the matrices $\widehat{F}_{i}:\mathbb{K}^{\Sigma^{*}\times n}$ and $\widehat{B}_{i}:\mathbb{K}^{n\times\Sigma^{*}}$ with $\widehat{F}_{i}[w,\cdot]=\alpha_{i}M_{i}(w)$ and $\widehat{B}_{i}[\cdot,w]=M_{i}(w)\eta_{i}$ for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$.
L221: It follows from minimality and cite30†Proposition 3.1 that $\widehat{F}_{i}$ and $\widehat{B}_{i}$ have full rank $n$. Thus, there is an invertible matrix $Q\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}$ with $\widehat{F}_{1}=\widehat{F}_{2}Q$. We show that $\mathcal{A}_{2}$ is the forward conjugate of $\mathcal{A}_{1}$ with base $Q$.
L222: We have $\alpha_{1}=\widehat{F}_{1}[\varepsilon,\cdot]=\widehat{F}_{2}[\varepsilon,\cdot]Q=\alpha_{2}Q$. Letting $H$ denote the Hankel matrix of $\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{1}\rrbracket=\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{2}\rrbracket$, we have $\widehat{F}_{2}Q\widehat{B}_{1}=\widehat{F}_{1}\widehat{B}_{1}=H=\widehat{F}_{2}\widehat{B}_{2}$. Since $\widehat{F}_{2}$ has full rank, it follows that $Q\widehat{B}_{1}=\widehat{B}_{2}$.
L223: In particular, $Q\eta_{1}=Q\widehat{B}_{1}[\cdot,\varepsilon]=\widehat{B}_{2}[\cdot,\varepsilon]=\eta_{2}$.
L224: For any $a\in\Sigma$ let $H_{a}\in\mathbb{K}^{\Sigma^{*}\times\Sigma^{*}}$ be the matrix with $H_{a}[x,y]=\llbracket\mathcal{A}_{i}\rrbracket(xay)$ for all $x,y\in\Sigma^{*}$. We have $\widehat{F}_{i}M_{i}(a)\widehat{B}_{i}=H_{a}$ for all $a\in\Sigma$. Thus, for all $a\in\Sigma$ we have:
L225: 
L226:  | $$\widehat{F}_{2}QM_{1}(a)\widehat{B}_{1}\ =\ \widehat{F}_{1}M_{1}(a)\widehat{B}_{1}\ =\ H_{a}\ =\ \widehat{F}_{2}M_{2}(a)\widehat{B}_{2}\ =\ \widehat{F}_{2}M_{2}(a)Q\widehat{B}_{1}$$  |
L227: Since $\widehat{F}_{2}$ and $\widehat{B}_{1}$ have full rank, we have $QM_{1}(a)=M_{2}(a)Q$ for all $a\in\Sigma$. ∎

L231: ## 4 The Hankel Automaton
L232: 
L233: Let $s:\Sigma^{*}\to\mathbb{K}$ be a series of rank $n$, with Hankel matrix $H$. Call a set $C=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}$ complete if the columns of $H[\cdot,C]$ form a basis of the column space of $H$.
L234: 
L235: Note that for any $G\subseteq\Sigma^{*}$ and $w\in\Sigma^{*}$ we have $H[Gw,C]=H[G,wC]$, where $Gw:=\{gw\mid g\in G\}$ and $wC:=\{wc\mid c\in C\}$.
L236: Let $C=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{n}\}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}$ be complete. Then, for any $w\in\Sigma^{*}$ there is a unique column vector $\eta_{w}\in\mathbb{K}^{n}$ with $H[\cdot,w]=H[\cdot,C]\eta_{w}$, and for all $a\in\Sigma$ a unique matrix $\overline{M}(a)\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}$ with $H[\cdot,C]\overline{M}(a)=H[\cdot,aC]$. We define the Hankel automaton for $s,C$ as $\overline{\mathcal{A}}=(n,\Sigma,\overline{M},H[\varepsilon,C],\eta_{\varepsilon})$.
L237: ###### Proposition 4.1.
L238: 
L239: Let $\overline{\mathcal{A}}=(n,\Sigma,\overline{M},H[\varepsilon,C],\eta_{\varepsilon})$ be the Hankel automaton for $s,C$. Then for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$ we have $H[\cdot,C]\overline{M}(w)=H[\cdot,wC]$. Hence, if $G=\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{n}\}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}$ is such that $H[G,C]$ has full rank, we have $\overline{M}(w)=H[G,C]^{-1}H[G,wC]$ for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$.
L240: ###### Proof.
L241: 
L242: We proceed by induction on the length of $w$. The induction base ($w=\varepsilon$) is trivial. For the step, let $w\in\Sigma^{*}$ and $a\in\Sigma$. We have:
L243: 
L244:  | $\displaystyle H[\cdot,C]\overline{M}(wa)\ $  | $\displaystyle=\ H[\cdot,wC]\overline{M}(a)$  |  | induction hypothesis  |
L245:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ H[\cdot\,w,C]\overline{M}(a)$  |
L246:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ H[\cdot\,w,aC]$  |  | definition of $\overline{M}(a)$  |
L247:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ H[\cdot,waC]$  |
L248: ###### Proposition 4.2.
L249: 
L250: Let $\overline{\mathcal{A}}=(n,\Sigma,\overline{M},H[\varepsilon,C],\eta_{\varepsilon})$ be the Hankel automaton for $s,C$. Then $\llbracket\overline{\mathcal{A}}\rrbracket=s$ and $\overline{\mathcal{A}}$ is minimal.
L251: ###### Proof.
L252: 
L253: We have for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$:
L254: 
L255:  | $\displaystyle\llbracket\overline{\mathcal{A}}\rrbracket(w)\ $  | $\displaystyle=\ H[\varepsilon,C]\overline{M}(w)\eta_{\varepsilon}$  |
L256:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ H[\varepsilon,wC]\eta_{\varepsilon}$  |
L257:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ H[w,C]\eta_{\varepsilon}$  |
L258:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ H[w,\varepsilon]$  |  | definition of $\eta_{\varepsilon}$  |
L259:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ s(w)$  |
L260: 
L261: Minimality follows from cite27†Proposition 3.3 . ∎

L263: 
L264: Let $s:\Sigma^{*}\to\mathbb{K}$ be a series and $n\in\mathbb{N}$. Then $\textup{rank}(s)\leq n$ if and only if there is an automaton $\mathcal{A}$ with $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket=s$ that has at most $n$ states.
L265: 
L266: ###### Proof.
L267: 
L268: Follows from cite39†Propositions 4.2 and cite27†3.3 . ∎
L269: 
L270: The following proposition uses some notions from cite8†Section 3 .
L271: ###### Proposition 4.4.
L272: 
L273: Let $\mathcal{A}=(n,\Sigma,M,\alpha,\eta)$ be forward-minimal. Let $C=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}\}\subseteq\Sigma^{*}$ be such that the columns of the matrix $B:=(M(c_{1})\eta,\ldots,M(c_{r})\eta)\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times r}$ form a basis of the backward space of $\mathcal{A}$. Then $C$ is complete, and the backward conjugate of $\mathcal{A}$ with base $B$ is the Hankel automaton for $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket,C$.
L274: ###### Proof.
L275: Let $H$ be the Hankel matrix of $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket$. Let $\widehat{F}:\mathbb{K}^{\Sigma^{*}\times n}$ and $\widehat{B}:\mathbb{K}^{n\times\Sigma^{*}}$ be the matrices with $\widehat{F}[w,\cdot]=\alpha M(w)$ and $\widehat{B}[\cdot,w]=M(w)\eta$ for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$. We have $\widehat{F}\widehat{B}=H$ and $\widehat{B}[\cdot,C]=B$, hence $\widehat{F}B=H[\cdot,C]$. Since the column spaces of $\widehat{B}$ and $B$ are equal, it follows that the column spaces of $H$ and $H[\cdot,C]$ are equal.
L276: Since $\mathcal{A}$ is forward-minimal, $\widehat{F}$ has full rank. Thus, $r=\textup{rank}(B)=\textup{rank}(\widehat{F}B)=\textup{rank}(H[\cdot,C])$, so the columns of $H[\cdot,C]$ are linearly independent. Hence, $C$ is complete.
L277: Let $\overline{\mathcal{A}}=(r,\Sigma,\overline{M},H[\varepsilon,C],\eta_{\varepsilon})$ be the Hankel automaton for $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket,C$. For all $x,w\in\Sigma^{*}$ and all $c\in C$ we have $\widehat{F}[x,\cdot]M(w)B[\cdot,c]=\alpha M(x)M(w)M(c)\eta=\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket(xwc)=H[x,wc]$. Thus, we have for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$:
L278:  | $\displaystyle\widehat{F}M(w)B\ $  | $\displaystyle=\ H[\cdot,wC]$  |
L279:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ H[\cdot,C]\overline{M}(w)$  |
L280:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ \widehat{F}B\overline{M}(w)$  |
L281: Since $\widehat{F}$ has full rank, it follows that $M(w)B=B\overline{M}(w)$ for all $w\in\Sigma^{*}$. Similarly, we have $\widehat{F}B\eta_{\varepsilon}=H[\cdot,C]\eta_{\varepsilon}=H[\cdot,\varepsilon]=\widehat{F}\eta$, and since $\widehat{F}$ has full rank, it follows that $B\eta_{\varepsilon}=\eta$. Finally, we have $\alpha B=\widehat{F}[\varepsilon,\cdot]B=H[\varepsilon,C]$. We conclude that $\overline{\mathcal{A}}$ is the backward conjugate of $\mathcal{A}$ with base $B$. ∎
L282: ###### Theorem 4.5.
L283: 
L284: Let $\mathcal{A}=(n,\Sigma,M,\alpha,\eta)$ be an automaton. One can compute in polynomial time (with $O(|\Sigma|n^{3})$ arithmetic operations) a complete (for $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket$) set $C=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}\}\subseteq\Sigma^{\leq r-1}$ with $r\leq n$, and the Hankel automaton for $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket,C$.
L285: ###### Proof.

L287: By the backward analogue of cite15†Proposition 2.1 one can compute in the required time a set $C=\{c_{1},\ldots,c_{r}\}\subseteq\Sigma^{\leq r-1}$ and the matrix $B=(\overrightarrow{M}(c_{1})\overrightarrow{\eta},\ldots,\overrightarrow{M}(c_{r})\overrightarrow{\eta})\in\mathbb{K}^{\overrightarrow{n}\times r}$ such that the columns of $B$ form a basis of the backward space of $\overrightarrow{\mathcal{A}}$. L288: By cite31†Proposition 3.2 , it can be computed in the required time. By cite40†Proposition 4.4 , $\mathcal{A}^{\prime}$ is the Hankel automaton for $\llbracket\mathcal{A}\rrbracket,C$. ∎

L292: ## 5 Computations in NC
L293: 
L294: We show that some of the mentioned polynomial-time computations can even be carried out in the complexity class NC, which comprises those languages having L-uniform Boolean circuits of polylogarithmic depth and polynomial size, or, equivalently, those problems solvable in polylogarithmic time on parallel random-access machines with polynomially many processors. We have $\text{NL}\subseteq\text{{NC}}\subseteq\text{P}$.
L295: ###### Lemma 5.1.
L296: 
L297: Let $A\in\mathbb{K}^{m\times n}$. The row spaces of $A$ and $A^{T}A$ are equal.
L298: ###### Proof.
L299: 
L300: It is clear that the row space of $A^{T}A$ is included in the row space of $A$. For the converse, it suffices to show that the null space of $A^{T}A$ is included in the null space of $A$. Let $x\in\mathbb{K}^{n}$ with $A^{T}Ax=0_{n}$, where $0_{n}$ denotes the zero vector. Then $(Ax)^{T}(Ax)=x^{T}A^{T}Ax=x^{T}0_{n}=0$, and hence $Ax=0_{m}$. ∎
L301: 
L302: In the following we assume $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}$.
L303: ###### Proposition 5.2 (cf. cite15†Proposition 2.1 ).
L304: 
L305: Let $\mathcal{A}=(n,\Sigma,M,\alpha,\eta)$ be an automaton. One can compute in NC a basis of the forward space $\mathcal{F}:=\langle\alpha M(w)\mid w\in\Sigma^{*}\rangle$.
L306: ###### Proof.
L307: 
L308: Let $F\in\mathbb{K}^{\Sigma^{\leq n-1}\times n}$ be the matrix with $F[w,\cdot]=\alpha M(w)$ for $w\in\Sigma^{\leq n-1}$. We have shown in cite7†Section 2 that the rows of $F$ span $\mathcal{F}$. By cite45†Lemma 5.1 , the rows of $E:=F^{T}F\in\mathbb{K}^{n\times n}$ also span $\mathcal{F}$.
L309: Let $e_{i}\in\{0,1\}^{n}$ denote the $i$th coordinate column vector, and let $\mathord{\otimes}$ denote the Kronecker product. Using the mixed-product property of $\mathord{\otimes}$ (i.e., $(AB)\otimes(CD)=(A\otimes C)(B\otimes D)$), we have:
L310:  | $\displaystyle E[i,j]\ $  | $\displaystyle=\ F^{T}[i,\cdot]\ F[\cdot,j]$  |
L311:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ \sum_{w\in\Sigma^{\leq n-1}}(\alpha M(w))[i]\ (\alpha M(w))[j]$  |
L312:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ \sum_{w\in\Sigma^{\leq n-1}}(\alpha M(w)e_{i})\otimes(\alpha M(w)e_{j})$  |
L313:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ \sum_{w\in\Sigma^{\leq n-1}}(\alpha\otimes\alpha)(M(w)\otimes M(w))(e_{i}\otimes e_{j})$  |
L314:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ (\alpha\otimes\alpha)\left(\sum_{w\in\Sigma^{\leq n-1}}M(w)\otimes M(w)\right)(e_{i}\otimes e_{j})$  |
L315:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ (\alpha\otimes\alpha)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{w\in\Sigma^{k}}M(w)\otimes M(w)\right)(e_{i}\otimes e_{j})$  |
L316:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ (\alpha\otimes\alpha)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sum_{a_{1}\cdots a_{k}\in\Sigma^{k}}(M(a_{1})\otimes M(a_{1}))\cdots(M(a_{k})\otimes M(a_{k}))\right)$  |
L317:  |  | $\displaystyle\qquad(e_{i}\otimes e_{j})$  |
L318:  |  | $\displaystyle=\ (\alpha\otimes\alpha)\left(\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\Big(\sum_{a\in\Sigma}M(a)\otimes M(a)\Big)^{k}\right)(e_{i}\otimes e_{j})$  |
L319: Since Kronecker products, sums and matrix powers [cite46†8 ] can be computed in NC, one can compute $E$ in NC. We include the $i$th row of $E$ in the desired basis of $\mathcal{F}$ if and only if $\textup{rank}(E[\{1,\ldots,i\},\cdot])>\textup{rank}(E[\{1,\ldots,i-1\},\cdot])$. This can be done in NC, as the rank of a matrix can be determined in NC [cite47†14 ]. ∎
